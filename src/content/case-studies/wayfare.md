## The problem

Booking a multi-city trip on the big aggregators is a game of hide-and-seek. The cheapest routes are often self-transfer combinations across airlines that the major sites bury, because they can't sell them as a single protected ticket. Travellers who find these routes save real money — but take on real risk if a leg is delayed and no one owns the connection.

Wayfare set out to surface those hidden routes **and** be honest about what they cost you in risk.

## What I designed

A conversational planning surface where you describe the trip in plain language, and Wayfare assembles candidate itineraries — including the cheaper multi-airline combinations. Every itinerary carries an explicit **risk cue**: how tight the connection is, whether bags transfer, and what happens if a leg slips.

- **Assistant / Manual toggle** so power users can drop into a traditional search when they want control.
- **Reasoning on tap** — every recommendation can be expanded to show *why* it was suggested.
- **Human-in-the-loop confirmation** — no booking is ever finalised without a person confirming the risky legs.

## Outcome

- 26 high-fidelity screens shipped across light and dark themes.
- A 5-point SUS lift in unmoderated testing versus the previous concept.
- The risk-cue pattern became the anchor of the product's trust story with early users.
